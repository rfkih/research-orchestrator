The win rate of 20.0% combined with an average win-to-loss ratio of 5.74:1 superficially resembles a crisis-alpha or tail-hedge payoff profile rather than a classical mean-reversion system. In a well-functioning mean-reversion strategy, win rates typically exceed 50% with contained win-to-loss ratios, because the thesis is that prices return to equilibrium reliably and frequently. Here the relationship inverts: entries occur only at extreme oversold readings where further adverse displacement is common, and the rare genuine recoveries produce outsized gains. The resulting positive return skewness of 1.344 is consistent with this interpretation. However, positive skewness alone confers no edge: the sample is far too small to distinguish a genuinely skewed-but-profitable distribution from one that will eventually exhaust its occasional winners against accumulated losses, and the quarterly record of zero winning trades from 2024Q4 onward suggests the latter is the operative dynamic.
