Systematic mean-reversion strategies on high-volatility cryptocurrency assets have attracted sustained research interest, yet empirical evidence for persistent, statistically significant edge remains scarce. Solana's native token (SOLUSDT) exhibits pronounced oscillatory dynamics on the four-hour bar, making it a natural candidate for oscillator-driven reversal systems. This study presents a rigorous empirical evaluation of the Mean Reversion Oscillator strategy (MRO) applied to SOLUSDT on the four-hour interval, motivated by two antecedent results within the same research programme: the outright discard of a momentum-reversal strategy (MMR-SOL-1h) applied to the one-hour cadence, and the borderline, inconclusive outcome of a dual-confirmation breakout variant (DCB-SOL-4h) on the same four-hour timeframe. The central hypothesis under investigation is that reducing entry complexity to a single-mechanism oscillator filter combining Bollinger Band lower-band deviation with RSI oversold confirmation can surface exploitable mean-reversion edge at the cadence where prior multi-component systems failed to achieve decisive statistical separation.
