The pivot from MMR-SOL-1h and DCB-SOL-4h to an oscillator-only mechanism was theoretically motivated by the hypothesis that removing dual-confirmation complexity would increase signal frequency while preserving directional quality. The data refute this hypothesis on its primary premise. The one-hour cadence of MMR-SOL-1h naturally generates more bars per unit time; migrating to the four-hour bar while imposing a strict RSI-25 threshold simultaneously reduces bar frequency and tightens the entry filter, suppressing signal generation below any actionable rate. Simplifying the entry mechanism did not produce the anticipated frequency improvement. It produced a strategy that fires so rarely that no form of statistical inference can determine whether the infrequent signals represent edge or randomness.
